# Thirteenth independent audit: M2.0 (`ed3a4e4`, 2026-09-25)

- **Auditor:** a fresh agent, with no author review to read.
- **Scope:** `5d16d80..ed3a4e4`, branch `m2/contracts`. The twelfth rework was examined
  hardest.
- **Method:**
  - CDK 0.18.1's swap saga, `check_state`, `add_proofs` and startup recovery, read from
    the cargo cache: the mint M2.1 is built on;
  - 20 new mutants (19 seeder, 1 watcher), each run against all 62 scenarios with
    controls: 8 caught, 12 survived, 9 of them against the spec, each with a paired
    repro;
  - 300,000 differential parser cases (100,000 of them on mint URLs): 0 disagreements,
    and all 37,883 accepted cases passed both round trips;
  - the lock job simulated on a pristine clone, which passed in 42.5 s;
  - the check job simulated on a pristine clone, with every variable `gitlab-ci.yml`
    sets, which passed in 443 s;
  - five lock routes in disposable clones: all refused, or failing closed where no job
    can reach them.
- **Controls:** 62/62 scenarios, 233/233 planted flaws and pay1 2/2.
- **sovtech's bar:** zero findings.
- **Verdict:** not ready to push. 8 findings: 4 Medium and 4 Low. The twelfth rework's
  rule held as the mock implemented it, and its read order is right against CDK. The
  gaps were in what the spec and the harness left open: CDK's pending state, learning
  that one account's unknown swap could delay, reads made under a lock no real engine
  can hold, and the cost of undecided swaps. The twelfth audit's resolutions, and the
  three things found while resolving them, held.
- **Not findings:**
  - the trade-offs NFX-07 states: an account refused while an outcome is genuinely
    unknown, the three tries a session and their reclaim fees, a lying seeder's one
    payment, and the seeder trusting its quoted mint's reads (a mint that lies about
    state could take the proofs outright);
  - three harmless mutants: deciding one second after the deadline (a takeover at the
    deadline abandons the swap first), skipping the restore when every input is unspent
    (a claim processed between the reads is learnt at the next read), and "every input
    spent" for "an input spent" (it differs only while a third party holds part of the
    token, and an honest watcher's reclaim claims the rest).

The resolutions are in `70626f4`.

**Reading a swap's state, as a real engine must.** The mock now learns outside its state
lock: it collects the undecided swaps, reads them all in one NUT-07 check and one NUT-09
restore, then applies each decision only to a swap still undecided. So an answer that
lands during the reads has settled its swap, and one swap still unknown delays no other.
A proof reserved by a request the mint is still processing reads `PENDING`: not spent.
The harness can now:
- hold a request after reserving its inputs, as CDK does before it signs (`hold_next_swap_reserving`), and roll such requests back, as CDK does at startup (`roll_back_reserved`);
- leave NUT-07 checks unanswered while restores are answered (`state_check_outage`);
- deliver held responses during a read (`deliver_responses_mid_read`);
- count the mint's state reads (`state_reads`).

**Found while resolving:** the expiry changed what one scenario pinned.
`only_never_paid_accounts_are_forgotten` kept a swap in flight past `account_ttl` with
its inputs unspent, which is exactly what now expires. Its payment's connection now
drops at once, so the account is idle from before the swap's deadline: it still pins
"an account holding an unknown swap is kept", within the expiry. Adding a waiting
`hello` to the never-lands case (#6) also masked the twelfth audit's
`DecidedKeepsTurn` (the hello took the dead turn over itself), so that case is now two
payments: one with a waiting hello and one without.
