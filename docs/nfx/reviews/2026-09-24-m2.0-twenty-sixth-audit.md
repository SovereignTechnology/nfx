The resolutions are in `cdab029` to `3347db8`. They were made in three groups on branches of their own,
each checked by an independent reviewer and corrected, then merged; the conversion of the

pinned what survived (below).

**Deadlines, on one clock.** A payment's answer is a refusal only if its checks reached
it by the deadline: keys that come in the deadline's second or later are not used, a
swap's outcome settled in that second is late, and a payment whose swap is not sent by
then is not rechecked. Each is answered `mint-unavailable`. A payment dropped before its
swap is sent is abandoned unswapped and frees the turn then. NFX-07 §3 and `session.rs`
say so; the mock follows it.

**Takeovers.** A payment or `hello` that takes a dead turn over is checked for its peer's
ban first, reads the abandoned swap, and reads as one that waited; a `hello` that comes
after the deadline that freed the turn did not wait. A `hello` checks the ban as it
arrives, before any wait or read, and again as it answers.

**Refusals keep nothing.** A refused `hello`, request or `pay` leaves no account, no place
under the session cap and no session id open, `mint-unavailable` included; the one
exception is a claim learnt later, which creates the account then. A banned peer is
refused `banned` before anything else it offers. An exact payment that would take an
account's `spent_total` past 2^53−1 is refused `overpaid`, since no `ack` could carry it.

**The watcher.** An `ack` at or below the ledger, in either field, is refused and stops
the watcher; nothing undoes a stop (an `ack`, a quote, a refused request or `hello`,
`mint-unavailable` with every proof back, the session's end, a new video's ledger). A
quote names the watcher's mint only by its exact URL. A reclaim that finds a proof spent
checks the proofs' states first and takes back the inputs left, outside a 12003 too
(NFX-07 §3a says so, and that CDK 0.18.1's own revoke does not). The mock's wallet selects
as CDK does, keeping good older proofs beside expired ones. Holding swaps holds the
seeder's own requests, on a slow link; third parties still reach the mint.

**The runner.** A panic counts as the suite failing only when the line of `adversary.rs`
it was raised on makes a check (an assertion, `panic!`, `unreachable!`, a named `expect`),
and its message is not one of Rust's runtime panics, now listed in full for the library's
caller-reporting checks. The suite's 303 bare `unwrap`s are named checks, each saying what
the suite requires at its line, and `clippy::unwrap_used` is denied in `adversary.rs`, so a
new one fails CI.

**Found while resolving:**
- The first fix for #4 pinned a refusal reached after the deadline by a payment left
  unpolled while its keys waited; its reviewer showed that contradicts NFX-07 as written.
  The clock is now the seeder's own, and that case is pinned neither way.
- `no_retry_at_the_deadline` still queues a mint event; the new deadline cases run before
  it.
- The watcher group's first text said the take-back happens "whether or not a keyset has
  expired", which overstated it after a 12003; it says "those not lost to the expiry".
- Merging the three groups needed hand merges where the turn's new shape (a takeover it
