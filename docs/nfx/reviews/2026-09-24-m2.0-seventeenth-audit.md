# Seventeenth independent audit: M2.0 (`01f9811`, 2026-09-25)

- **Auditor:** a fresh agent, with no author review to read.
- **Scope:** `81a692e..01f9811`, branch `m2/contracts`. The sixteenth rework was examined
  hardest, against CDK 0.18.1's swap saga, verification, signatory and error codes.
- **Method:**
  - 20 new seeder mutants, each run against all 62 scenarios with 10 controls: 8 caught,
    2 of them only by accident; 12 survived, 9 of them against the spec, each with a
    paired repro (11 repros, and one demonstration on an honest pair);
  - 600,000 differential parser cases over 4 seeds, with new families (loopback
    look-alikes, IPv6 zone ids, 5-digit ports, U+2028 and C1 controls, multibyte text at
    the 32 KiB edge, trailing commas, comments, lone surrogates): 0 disagreements, 0
    round-trip failures, 0 writer refusals;
  - the lock job simulated on a clone, which passed in 73.7 s;
  - the check job simulated on a clone, with every variable `gitlab-ci.yml` sets, which
    passed in 9 min 47 s;
  - 6 new lock routes in a disposable clone: all refused, or without effect.
- **Controls:** 62/62 scenarios, 275/275 planted flaws and pay1 2/2.
- **sovtech's bar:** zero findings.
- **Verdict:** not ready to push. 10 findings: 1 Medium and 9 Low. Five of the sixteenth
  rework's 7 resolutions held in full, and two (F1 and F7) in part. The worst gap: the
  deadline was pinned only for reads that start as the payment arrives. An engine that
  gave its reads their own 60 s passed every scenario, and after a 59 s key fetch, with
  the mint's read endpoints hanging, it answered at 119 s: an honest watcher stopped
  paying an honest seeder.
- **Not findings:**
  - the trade-offs NFX-07 states: a quote up to a second stale, a `hello` whose reads
    take their own time, reads scoped to one account, and an account blocked while one
    of its swaps is undecided;
  - three harmless mutants: a `hello` that reuses only another hello's read (the cap
    still holds), a split read asking at the deadline (it costs the mock no time), and
    a payment that awaits a round trip before its read (a legitimate engine shape);
  - an observation: the mock checked retired outputs and pending inputs before forged
    proofs, while CDK verifies inputs first. No scenario depends on it; the rework's new
    expired-keyset check follows CDK and comes first.

The resolutions are in `c906699`.

**Reads that are round trips.** The mock mint can now answer each read of swap state on
the reader's next poll, as a real mint's round trips do
(`MockHarness::with_round_trip_reads`). The honest engine then takes a read's place in
its second before sending it, so an entry that comes meanwhile reuses it. The whole suite
also runs against that harness (`tests/round_trip.rs`, whose run the lock counts), and
passes. One scenario required a freed `hello` to answer within one poll; it now allows a
few polls, and still no time.

**Keyset expiry, as CDK answers it.** The mock mint models an expired keyset (CDK 12003,
`MintEvent::ExpireKeyset`). A request spending an expired proof is refused, except one
whose inputs the mint reserved before the expiry: that one still signs. NFX-07 §3 now
settles every 12003 only once no input is pending, since the code does not say whose
keyset expired.

**Found while resolving:** the auditor's repro for finding 1 read the clock after the
harness's other future had run, which had moved it on. A flaw reading before its key
fetch answered at 60 s but measured 90 s, so it was caught only by accident. The
scenario now times the answer inside the payment's own future, and that flaw is caught
by its own assertion. A section comment still said "one read a second"; it says two.
