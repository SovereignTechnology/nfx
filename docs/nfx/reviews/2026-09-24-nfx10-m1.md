# Self-review: the NFX-10 M1 browser mesh, tracker and bridge (2026-09-24)

Scope: branch `nfx10/mesh`, `main..HEAD`.
- `nfx-node::tracker` (WebTorrent tracker) and `Tracker::local`.
- `nfx-node::p2pml` (the p2p-media-loader 4.0.0 data-channel codec).
- `nfx-node::bridge` (str0m WebRTC peer).
- `nfxd` `--embed-tracker`, `--bridge`, `--tracker-url`.
- `nfx-wasm` `streamSwarmId`.
- The player mesh (`web/player/src/mesh.ts`) and `e2e-mesh.ts`.
- NFX-10 (Draft) edits.

Method: `differential-review` (three new network-facing surfaces: a public WebSocket
server, a UDP WebRTC endpoint, and peer bytes in the browser) and `sharp-edges`. **This
is the author's own review. The two earlier audits each found what self-review missed,
so an independent audit is due before this is pushed.**

## Surfaces and their limits

| Surface | Who can reach it | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| Tracker (WebSocket, `--embed-tracker`) | anyone | Admission only for §2 infohashes of verified hash lists this node holds; deletion withdraws them. `ConnLimits` (1024 / 32 per IP). 64 KiB messages. ≤10 offers, 8 KiB SDP, re-serialised. `peer_id` bound to its socket, and a live one cannot be taken over. 60-message burst then 2/s, else closed. ≤16 swarms per socket, ≤1000 peers per swarm. Ping every 30 s, closed after 75 s of silence. No scrape. |
| Bridge (UDP, `--bridge`) | anyone who gets an offer relayed to it, i.e. a swarm member | A specific bind address is required. ≤64 connections, one per remote peer and swarm. Unopened after 20 s: dropped. ICE `disconnected` or channel close: dropped. Requests: 60 burst then 20/s, else dropped. One upload per peer (a new request cancels). Serves only store files, re-verified on read. **Never downloads from browsers.** |
| Player mesh (`&tracker=`) | the page's swarms | Every segment (P2P and HTTP) is checked by the WASM verifier before hls.js sees it, and byte ranges are refused. Playlists and inits stay on the verifying loader. Trackers are `wss:` or loopback `ws:` only, without credentials, query or fragment, at most 4. No ICE server by default. |

## Findings (fixed in this branch)

| # | Finding | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Worst-case tracker memory: 1024 sockets × 64 queued relays × 16 KiB SDP ≈ 1 GiB. | SDP cap 8 KiB, outbox 32, so about 256 MiB worst case. Reaching it takes hundreds of IPs, because of the 32-per-IP cap. |
| S2 | A browser could make the bridge read and re-hash a whole segment per request, unboundedly. | A per-peer request budget: 60 burst, 20/s, else dropped. A normal player makes one request at a time. |
| S3 | A string message from Rust would be misread by the browser as a zero-filled data chunk (protocol reference §C). | The bridge writes binary only. |
| S4 | A data chunk that happened to start with `cstr`/`dstr` and end with `cend`/`dend` would be read as a command. | `data_chunks` shortens such a chunk by a byte. Tested with a crafted segment. |

## Known limits (carried)

- **Bridge slots can be filled by fake offers.** A swarm member can relay offers that
  never open, holding the 64 slots 20 s at a time. Before ICE there is no address to
  limit by, because browsers present mDNS names. The node itself is unaffected, but its
  bridge capacity is not.
- **The mesh shows viewers' IP addresses to their peers.** That is inherent to WebRTC
  meshes, and is why the player joins only when given `&tracker=`. It sets no STUN
  server by default, so no third party learns who watches.
- **The bridge answers but never offers.** A browser reaches it at the browser's next
  announce (≤120 s) after the bridge joins; in the e2e that was immediate. Across real
  NATs a bridge needs a reachable address; STUN/TURN is untested (S3 caveat).
- **Trackers are found by configuration only.** The player does not yet read `webrtc`
  endpoints from beacons, because p2p-media-loader fixes its trackers when the stream
  starts.
- The mesh e2e needs Chromium and ffmpeg, so like the other e2e tests it is not in CI.

## Evidence

- Rust:
  - tracker tests: relay, the refusals bittorrent-tracker lacks (a `null` offer, a
    `peer_id` takeover, oversize, flood), and the local socket;
  - codec byte-identical to the library's own encoder on every command, including the
    256-member group and the forced split;
  - the daemon admits and withdraws swarms.
  - `check.sh` passes: 29 test suites, clippy, deny (str0m's tree is licence-clean) and
    WASM.
- `e2e-mesh.ts`, PASS 3/3 (plus one run after S1/S2):
  - a peer fed by a lying origin gives the real player about 901 KB over WebRTC, and the
    tampered segment is rejected exactly;
  - with the origin cut at 3 s, the viewer plays to 18 s on about 6.2 MB from the Rust
    bridge;
  - the tracker refuses a foreign swarm.
- `e2e.ts` (no tracker): PASS, unchanged.
