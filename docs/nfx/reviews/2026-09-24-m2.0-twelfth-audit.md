# Twelfth independent audit: M2.0 (`e894fc8`, 2026-09-25)

- **Auditor:** a fresh agent, with no author review to read.
- **Scope:** `5d16d80..e894fc8`, branch `m2/contracts`. The eleventh rework was examined
  hardest.
- **Method:**
  - 15 new mutants of the mock, each run against all 62 scenarios and the auditor's
    repro scenarios: 9 survived the suite, 6 of them against the spec, 2 equivalent and
    1 harmless. The honest mock passes every paired repro;
  - a new harness option, `time_out_next_swap`: the seeder's client gives up on a
    request that stays queued at the mint;
  - 450,000 differential parser cases (150,000 of them on mint URLs): 0 disagreements,
    and all 40,587 accepted cases passed both round trips;
  - the lock job simulated on a pristine clone, which passed in 42 s;
  - the check job simulated on a pristine clone, with every variable `gitlab-ci.yml`
    sets, which passed in 406 s;
  - eight lock routes in disposable clones: all refused, and the stand-in shared source
    directory was left intact.
- **Controls:** 62/62 scenarios, 220/220 planted flaws and pay1 2/2.
- **sovtech's bar:** zero findings.
- **Verdict:** not ready to push. 7 findings: 4 Medium and 3 Low. Two are protocol gaps
  in the eleventh rework's new rule, and five are behaviour the suite did not pin. The
  eleventh audit's resolutions #3 to #9 held; #1 and #2 only in part (#4 to #6 below).
- **Not findings:**
  - the trade-offs NFX-07 states: an account refused while an outcome is genuinely
    unknown, the three tries a session and their reclaim fees, one payment lost to a
    quote then refused, a lying seeder's one payment;
  - two equivalent mutants: counting only abandoned swaps in flight (turns are
    serialised, so another payment's swap in flight is past its deadline), and checking
    without excepting the asking payment (its own swap is never sent at that moment);
  - one harmless mutant: learning only at arrival, not again at the send, which costs
    one extra `mint-unavailable` in a narrow window and breaks nothing the spec requires.

The resolutions are in `2d6de6d`.

**When a swap's outcome is known.** The eleventh rework ended "in flight" only when an
answer came, so a request the mint held unprocessed blocked its account for as long as it
hung. And a restore finding the outputs unsigned was taken as "nothing", although the
request could still be processed. Both now follow one rule (NFX-07 §3, late outcomes). A
swap with no answer, lost or abandoned in flight past its deadline, is read on the next
entry while the mint is reachable: its inputs first (NUT-07), then its own outputs (NUT-09
restore).
- Outputs signed: a claim, credited late.
- Outputs unsigned with an input spent: nothing. The swap is atomic, so it can no longer
  go through.
- Outputs unsigned with every input unspent: still unknown, and read again later.

An honest watcher reclaims a payment answered `mint-unavailable`, which spends its inputs,
so its next payment finds the block cleared. A watcher that never reclaims stays bounded
by one unknown swap. Once decided, a swap's record goes: an answer that comes later changes
nothing, and a turn it still holds is freed. No client timeout is needed to end "in
flight": the reads end it.

**Found while resolving, and pinned:**
- **The order of the two reads.** A swap processed between them must show as signed. A
  second harness option, `process_timed_out_mid_read`, has the mint process a request the
  seeder gave up on right after the next read of a swap's state.
  `a_late_outcome_is_credited_never_banned` asserts the claim credited (4, 4). Flaw
  `RestoreBeforeInputs` (outputs read first) loses it.
- **A decided swap's late answer.** A lost answer arriving after a restore credited the
  claim must not be learnt again. Asserted (4, 4), where flaw `DecidedLostRelearnt` gives
  (4, 8).
- **A decided swap's turn.** A swap that never lands, decided without an answer, must free
  the turn it holds, or its account's state is never forgotten. The flood in
  `bans_expire_and_state_stays_bounded` now ends with such a payment, and nothing is
  held afterwards. Flaw `DecidedKeepsTurn`.
- **What earlier scenarios pinned.** A restore now shows a claim before its answer comes.
  Where a scenario was written for the answer's landing (the global cap staying full
  until then, the send-time rechecks for `stale` and `overpaid`, the late-mint pair's
  first quote), restores go unanswered for that step, so it still pins what it was
  written for. A new case asserts the restore path itself: the cap freed before the
  answer comes, and credited once when it does.
