- **Method:**

- **Verdict:** not ready to push. 8 findings: 1 Medium, 6 Low and 1 Info. Six of the

  worst gap was a rule that was wrong: CDK 0.18.1's restore leaves out every signature

- **Not findings:**

  - one harmless mutant: a first attempt refused 12003 settled as a retry is (for an
    honest payer no input is pending then, so the outcome is the same, at two extra
    reads).

The resolutions are in `a935362`.

**Expiry, as CDK has it.** The mock mint now models what CDK 0.18.1 does:
- `MintEvent::ExpireKeyset` and `Harness::expire_keyset` expire the mint's keyset, the
  seeder's outputs included;
- `MintEvent::ExpireInputKeyset` expires an older keyset of the payer's, while the
  outputs' keyset stays current;
- a request whose outputs' keyset has expired is refused, even one whose inputs the mint
  reserved before;
- a restore no longer shows an expired keyset's outputs;
- a watcher's reclaim of expired proofs is refused, and a NUT-07 check then says what
  became of them.

NFX-07 §3 decides a swap whose outputs' keyset has expired by the NUT-07 check alone:
- an input spent is the claim: the seeder's own request is the one known to have reached
  the mint, an honest watcher reclaims only after its answer, and no peer can make a
  keyset expire;
- every input unspent is nothing.

The seeder also derives outputs only from a keyset at least twice `account_ttl` from its
`final_expiry`, so an honest mint never puts it there.

**Found while resolving:**
- With the seeder's outputs expiring too, the rework's reserved-request cases would no
  longer sign; they now expire the payer's older keyset (`ExpireInputKeyset`), which is
  what they were written for. The first-attempt 12003 case runs both events, since with
  the mint's own keyset expired a planted `FirstExpiredUnknown` is decided at the next
  read and escaped otherwise.
- A planted `ExpiredRestoreDownKnown` first took a pending input for an unanswered
  restore, and so was caught by the wrong assertion; it is now the unanswered restore
  only.
